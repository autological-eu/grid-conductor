# Toward a €5 billion European investment allocation

## Current position

The retained `mixed_curve_fitting` physical joint LP can reclear 2025 solar, wind and declared existing-link changes together in about 30–40 seconds per full year. A separate **one-battery** chronological 48-hour look-ahead path reuses a HiGHS model and finishes 365 days in about 106 seconds for the 500 MW DE-LU reference. Its generation, network, energy stock and no-emergency gates pass, but it is **not yet integrated with arbitrary generator/link portfolios or multiple batteries**. It is a rolling policy with 48-hour perfect retrospective model inputs, not a single full-year storage optimum.

The 2025 PyPSA-Eur technology-cost source cached at `data/pypsa-eur/online-source-audit/costs_2025-v0.15.0.csv` contains 2025-currency-year investment entries: utility solar **594.8646 €/kW**, onshore wind **1,438.8845 €/kW**, battery inverter **287.4657 €/kW** and battery energy **250.0283 €/kWh**. Its source rows cite Danish Energy Agency technology catalogues. These are European technology assumptions, **not** site-specific buildable-project quotes. Existing-link envelope releases in the dispatch runner are historical-flow proxies, not yet costable physical line builds. Site buildability, grid connection, land/seabed route, permitting and country-specific CAPEX remain unresolved.

Consequently, a claimed **optimal €5 billion allocation would be premature**. A feasible-search prototype can use those costs as *explicit research assumptions*, but it must not disguise missing siting or line physics as an investable result. We can first optimize within a declared finite candidate set and disclose its scope; a global Europe-wide optimum needs stronger evidence and a certified bound on the search.

## First direct diminishing-return test

The same retained 2025 physical network, bids, fixed hydro and 48-hour rolling policy were rerun for DE-LU batteries of 1 GW/4 GWh and 2 GW/8 GWh. Each size keeps 95% charge/discharge efficiency, half-full initial and look-ahead terminal stock, 24-hour executed intervals and chronological carry. The 500 MW/2 GWh retained source-hashed run is the control. For the two fresh cases, all 8,760 executed hours passed the reusable solver's network, stock, no-emergency and no-simultaneous-cycling gates; maximum primal residual was **1.21×10⁻⁷** and **3.60×10⁻⁶ MW or MWh**, respectively, below the unchanged 10⁻⁴ gate. Their full-year solves took 78.4 and 82.0 seconds. Large stock/dispatch receipts and source-hashed summaries remain ignored under `data/bidding-zone-network-2025/storage-size-ladder-v1/`. Source: `baselines/compact/storage_size_ladder.py`.

| DE-LU battery | Assumed investment | 2025 modeled offer-cost reduction | Gross annual saving / assumed CAPEX |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 500 MW / 2 GWh, retained control | 643.79 M€ | 70.34 M€ | 10.93% |
| 1 GW / 4 GWh | 1,287.58 M€ | 138.86 M€ | 10.78% |
| 2 GW / 8 GWh | 2,575.16 M€ | 270.45 M€ | 10.50% |

At fixed four-hour duration, the first 500 MW block saves **70.34 M€/year** in the model, the second adds **68.52 M€/year**, and each of the next two adds an average **65.79 M€/year**. This is measurable local diminishing return. It does **not** show the best location or that four-hour batteries should consume any particular share of €5 billion. These are modeled *system offer-cost* savings, not battery owner profit, ENTSO-E congestion rent or net welfare; CAPEX is shown as a denominator only. Fixed-hydro response, forecast uncertainty, O&M, degradation and network-construction effects are excluded.

## Allocation objective and search scheme

Declare candidate blocks by zone, technology and size: source-profile solar/wind MW, batteries with independently costed MW and MWh, and only lines with a physical route, network mapping and cost. Apply zone-specific build caps and incompatibility rules **before** optimization. For a portfolio `S`, reclear all 8,760 hours jointly and define `B(S) = C(∅) − C(S)`, where `C` is the executed annual model offer cost, including battery throughput cost. For a €5 billion capital constraint, the first transparent research objective is **maximize `B(S)` subject to ΣCAPEX(S) ≤ €5 billion**. Separately report investor income, fixed O&M and discounted benefit-minus-CAPEX ranges; repeating one observed 2025 year for future decades is only a sensitivity, not a forecast. Annual cost saving is not equivalent to social welfare if demand or non-energy externalities change.

1. **Prepare a reviewable candidate table.** Give every block cost source/year, full hourly original availability, battery power/energy/duration/efficiency, physical zone, build cap and constraint provenance. No dispatch-derived renewable availability. Exclude commercial-envelope releases without a physical line model and route CAPEX from the funded set.
2. **Extend the coupled LP.** Generalize the reusable 48-hour template to several batteries and source-profile generator/link deltas in the *same* solve, with each stock carried day to day. Check zero-asset parity, single-battery parity, whole-year energy/water/network/no-emergency gates and terminal policy. Bound dual/stock non-uniqueness before relying on price or owner-revenue comparisons.
3. **Search on marginal response, always reclearing.** Start empty, evaluate affordable candidate blocks, take the best measured `Δ_i(S) = B(S ∪ {i}) − B(S)` per euro, then recompute every remaining marginal under the new joint portfolio. Include larger discrete blocks and battery duration alternatives. Do not sum solo benefits. Search several seeds and run drop/add and swap moves to escape greedy choices. Cache by exact portfolio identity and source hashes; use parallelism only for independent candidate replays after checking live jobs.
4. **Assess optimum scope.** Record the best feasible portfolio, budget use and a frontier across several budgets. This is a *best-found* allocation. Claim optimality only for an exhaustively evaluated small candidate set or with a valid mathematical upper bound/certificate. Diminishing returns in one ladder do not prove global submodularity; network complementarity and storage interactions can make greedy search arbitrarily misleading.
5. **Stress-test the shortlist.** Reclear under CAPEX ranges, battery terminal/look-ahead policies, source-availability and congestion-capacity sensitivities, and alternate weather/market years when available. Compare joint benefits, zonal prices, technology mix, flows, hydro stock and adverse effects, with every original numerical gate unchanged.

At current measured runtimes, 100 full-year renewable portfolios would cost roughly an hour of LP time; battery portfolios can take about 1–2 minutes each even with matrix reuse. A staged marginal search and a small exact shortlist are practical. A continent-wide combinatorial guarantee and a credible investor allocation are not yet supported by the current candidate/cost/solver scope.
